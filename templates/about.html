Introduction

My work sits where three interests overlap. Physics taught me to reduce a problem to the variables that matter and to distrust an answer I can't derive. Software gave me a way to turn ideas into tools other people can use. Security is the discipline that asks what happens when those tools meet someone who wants to misuse them.

I keep this site as a record of that work: the apps I build, the physics I'm studying, and notes written to make sure I actually understand something.

Cybersecurity

Security page

I'm interested in information security from both sides: understanding how systems are attacked, and designing software so that a compromise costs as little as possible. In practice that means encrypting data at rest, requesting only the permissions an app truly needs, and drawing hard lines around authentication and payment.

I'm also interested in security education — explaining risks clearly enough that people change what they do. I've taught data safety to university students through the ActionAid-backed Data Shield project, and in October 2026 I earned the Certified Cybersecurity Educator Professional (CCEP) credential from Red Team Leaders.

Software development

Projects

Most of what I build is for Android, in Kotlin with Jetpack Compose. I favour offline-first designs: apps that keep working without a connection and keep personal data on the device. WordSmrt and SmartLedger are the clearest examples. I also write small browser extensions and tools when something repetitive deserves automating.

Physics & research interests

Physics page

Beyond coursework, I'm drawn to condensed matter and semiconductor physics, and especially to materials for solar energy conversion such as perovskites. Computational methods — simulation and, increasingly, machine learning applied to materials — are where my physics and programming meet. These are interests I'm working towards, not completed research.
